# Recommendation purpose and measured progress

The purpose sentence describes the existing plan; it does not select a new workout or alter ladder, load, rest, or zone rules. Ladder advancement means progression, repeat/incomplete means consistency, and recalibration/down-step means rebuilding consistency. Without a ladder, cold-start/boundary/coverage probes are labeled evidence collection; other recommendations are described as capacity building without asserting an optimal adaptation target.

Analysis compares each grip and hand separately using valid measured session openers. The latest measured opener defines the comparison neighborhood: durations within a maximum/minimum ratio of 1.10 for force progress, or measured force within that ratio for hold-duration progress. Available setup identifiers must match. Historical best is the highest recorded outcome within this comparison neighborhood, not a fitted potential or global best across unlike efforts.

Recent performance is the median of the latest three comparable sessions versus the preceding three within 90 days, with at least one observation in the last 30 days. Fewer than six sessions is insufficient evidence. More than 5% upward change is labeled improving; more than 5% downward change is lower recently; smaller changes are broadly unchanged. These engineering thresholds provide descriptive summaries, not statistical significance or causal attribution. No interpolation or fatigue-normalized predictions are used.

Repeated effort compares second-rep duration divided by opening duration. It additionally requires valid consecutive recovery evidence, comparable measured force and opener duration, and actual rest within 10% (at least one second tolerance). Legacy planned rest cannot substitute for actual rest. Best-ever values appear separately from recent medians. Old measured averages remain available with a measurement-uncertainty note.

Limits: unknown historical setup, climbing exposure, and warm-up conditions cannot be verified; similar is not identical force/duration. These comparisons do not isolate adaptation from fatigue or other circumstances. The view is a compact way to inspect existing results, not a new benchmark protocol.
